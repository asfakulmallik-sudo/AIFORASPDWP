# Copilot Prompting Starter Guide

**For:** FinBridge office staff (Outlook, Word, and Excel users)
**From:** IT / DWP Engineering Team
**Purpose:** A quick, plain-English guide to help you get useful results from Microsoft 365 Copilot — no technical background needed.

---

## Why this guide exists

Copilot is a bit like a very fast, very literal new colleague. It can save you a lot of time, but only if you tell it clearly what you want. Vague requests get vague answers. This guide gives you a simple formula and real examples so you can start getting good results today.

---

## The "GPS" Prompt Formula

You don't need to remember any technical rules — just remember **GPS**, like the satnav in your car. Give Copilot a destination and the details, and it'll get you there.

| Letter | Stands for | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **G** | **Goal** | Say exactly what you want Copilot to do (summarise, draft, rewrite, calculate, explain). |
| **P** | **Provide context** | Give it the detail it needs — which email, which document, which data, who it's for. |
| **S** | **Specify format** | Tell it how you want the answer back (bullet points, a table, a short paragraph, a formula, a tone). |

**Example using GPS:**
> "**Summarise** (Goal) this email thread from Sarah about the Q3 budget (**Provide context**) in 3 bullet points, flagging anything that needs my sign-off (**Specify format**)."

If your prompt only has a Goal ("summarise this"), Copilot has to guess the rest — so it's worth the extra ten seconds to add the P and the S.

## Outlook — example prompts

Look for the Copilot icon near the top of Outlook (usually a small sparkle ✨, sometimes labelled "Copilot"). You can either type straight into it, or open the email or thread you want help with first and then ask — there's no "right" way to start, so don't worry about getting the order wrong.

1. **Summarising a long thread**
   > "Summarise this email thread in 5 bullet points, and highlight any actions that have been assigned to me."

2. **Drafting a reply**
   > "Draft a polite reply confirming I can attend the meeting on Thursday at 2pm, and ask if there's anything I need to prepare beforehand."

3. **Writing a new email**
   > "Write a short, friendly email to the finance team letting them know the month-end deadline has moved from the 25th to the 28th."

---

## Word — example prompts

Look for the same Copilot sparkle icon ✨ near the top of Word. You can use it on a blank page if you're starting something new, or open an existing document first if you want Copilot to work with what's already written — either way is fine.

1. **Summarising a document**
   > "Summarise this report into a one-page briefing with headings for Background, Key Findings, and Recommendations."

2. **Simplifying language**
   > "Rewrite this paragraph in plain English, suitable for someone with no finance background."

3. **Drafting a new document**
   > "Draft a one-page guide explaining how to submit an expense claim, using numbered steps and a friendly tone."

---

## Excel — example prompts

Look for the Copilot sparkle icon ✨ near the top of Excel. It helps to have the sheet or cells you're asking about open on screen, so Copilot knows what data you mean — but if you're not sure how to "select" anything, don't worry, just ask your question and Copilot will usually guide you from there.

1. **Explaining a formula**
   > "Explain what the formula in cell D2 is doing, in plain English."

2. **Summarising data**
   > "Summarise the trend in the Sales column over the last 6 months in 3 bullet points."

3. **Building a formula**
   > "Write a formula that adds up all the values in column C, but only where column B says 'Approved'."

## A note on keeping data safe (this isn't a reason to avoid Copilot!)

First, the reassuring bit: **you're encouraged to use Copilot in Outlook, Word, and Excel for your everyday work.** These are approved, safe tools that stay inside FinBridge's own protected systems — nothing you type into them leaves the building, so to speak.

The one habit worth remembering (it's the same one from Day 1 of this training): **only put confidential client or personal information into these approved, in-house Copilot tools — never into a public AI tool.**

- ✅ **Safe to use freely:** Copilot inside Outlook, Word, and Excel.
- 🚫 **Don't use for FinBridge work:** personal AI accounts or free AI tools you find online (like a personal ChatGPT login) — these sit outside FinBridge's systems, so client names, account numbers, case details, or figures shouldn't go into them.
- 🤔 **Not sure?** Just ask IT. Checking first is always the right move, and nobody will think it's a silly question.

**Quick check before you paste anything into any AI tool:** *Would I be comfortable if this appeared in a public search result? If not, don't paste it.*

## Getting better results — quick tips

- Be specific: "Summarise in 3 bullet points" works better than "summarise this."
- Tell Copilot who the output is for (e.g. "for someone with no finance background") — it changes the tone and detail.
- If the first answer isn't quite right, just ask again with more detail rather than starting over — Copilot remembers the conversation.
- You can always ask Copilot to redo something "shorter," "more formal," or "as a table" — refining is normal and expected.
